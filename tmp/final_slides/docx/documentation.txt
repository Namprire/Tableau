1. Unified Dataset Strategy
Recommended unit of analysis: one occupation-task pair, uniquely identified by soc_code × task_id. This is the only level that preserves both occupation information and task information in the same row. It can be aggregated upward to the occupation, task, occupational family, Job Zone, and salary-group levels without losing the within-occupation task structure.
The unified dataset replaces the earlier four-file workflow. The research questions and sub-questions remain substantively unchanged; only the data architecture and Tableau aggregation rules change.

2. Core Measurement Logic
AI exposure answers “How much is AI used?” Automation and augmentation orientation answer “When AI is used, is AI doing the work or helping a person do the work?”

Construction
Interpretation
Occupation AI exposure
Sum task_percentage_job across all tasks belonging to the same soc_code.
Share of all observed AI conversations attributed to that occupation.
Task automation score
directive + feedback_loop
Strength of automation-oriented interaction modes for a job-task pair.
Task augmentation score
task_iteration + validation + learning
Strength of augmentation-oriented interaction modes for a job-task pair.

task_automation_score / task_interaction_score_total

Task augmentation share
task_augmentation_score / task_interaction_score_total
Relative augmentation share among the five recorded interaction modes.

task_percentage_job × task_augmentation_share

AI role balance
augmentation orientation − automation orientation
Ranges from −1 (automation) to +1 (augmentation).

Occupation-level variables repeat across tasks within the same occupation; task-level variables may repeat across occupations sharing the same task. 5. Tableau Rules for Using One Dataset
Because the dataset is stored at the occupation-task level, some task-level and occupation-level fields repeat across rows. Correct aggregation is therefore essential.
Chart type
Rule
Example
Occupation-level ranking or scatterplots
Place soc_code on Detail. Use MIN or AVG of precomputed occupation-level variables, not SUM.
MIN([occupation_ai_exposure_pct])
Occupation-level boxplots by Job Zone
Each mark must represent one occupation. Put soc_code on Detail and use MIN/AVG of occupation-level measures.
MIN([occupation_ai_exposure_pct])
Task-level ranking
Place task_id on Detail. Use MIN or AVG of task_percentage_all because it repeats across occupations sharing the task.
MIN([task_percentage_all])
Overall automation versus augmentation
Use row-level exposure components and sum them across valid Q2 observations.
SUM([task_automation_exposure_pct]) / SUM([q2_classified_exposure_pct])
Five-part interaction profile
Sum each interaction exposure and divide by classified exposure at the chosen grouping level.
SUM([directive_exposure_pct]) / SUM([q2_classified_exposure_pct])
Counts of occupations or tasks
Use COUNTD on identifiers or SUM of the helper row flags.
COUNTD([soc_code]); COUNTD([task_id])
Job-family averages
Choose whether each occupation should receive equal weight or exposure weight. State the choice in the chart subtitle.
Equal occupation weight: AVG of one value per soc_code; exposure weight: sum exposure components.

7. Final Recommendation
Use analysis_dataset.csv as the only data source in Tableau.
Keep the grain at one unique occupation-task pair.
Use occupation-level precomputed variables for occupation questions, but aggregate them with MIN/AVG and one mark per soc_code.
Use task_percentage_all for task questions, but aggregate with MIN/AVG and one mark per task_id.
Use row-level exposure components for overall automation, augmentation, and five-part interaction profiles.
Always display exposure, valid task count, and classification coverage beside orientation rankings to avoid overinterpreting small or weakly classified occupations.
